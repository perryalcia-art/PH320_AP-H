THING 1

Does Your Writing Hand Beat Your Other Hand?

What We Did

Our class counted how many times each student could write “I am amazing” in 30 seconds with their writing hand and their other hand. We also recorded each student's yes-or-no answer to whether they write in script. Dr. Jacques's writing-hand count of 8 was the benchmark. We used a one-sample t-test to compare the class writing-hand average with the benchmark, an independent-samples t-test to compare writing-hand counts between the yes and no groups, and a paired-samples t-test to compare each student's two hands.

My Results

Test Mean 1 Mean 2 Difference p-value
One-sample: writing hand vs. benchmark 6.33 8.00 −1.67 0.00105
Independent-samples: yes vs. no 6.40 6.25 0.15 0.848
Paired-samples: writing hand vs. other hand 6.33 2.56 3.78 0.00000647

The Three Write-Ups

Writing hand vs. benchmark: The class average was 1.67 fewer phrases than the professor's benchmark. This difference was statistically significant (p = 0.00105).

Yes vs. no groups: The yes group averaged 0.15 more phrases than the no group. This difference was not statistically significant (p = 0.848).

Writing hand vs. other hand: Students averaged 3.78 more phrases with their writing hand. This difference was statistically significant (p = 0.00000647).

In Plain Words

In this class, students wrote the phrase more times with their writing hand than with their other hand. The yes and no groups had very similar writing-hand averages, while the class writing-hand average was lower than the professor's benchmark.

What I Would Do Differently

This was a small class sample, so the results may not represent other students. The yes and no groups were also small and unequal in size. I would include more students and make sure everyone follows the same timing and counting instructions.
